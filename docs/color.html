Signal uses eight colors, grouped into three tiers. The palette is warm throughout: cold white and pure black were rejected as too clinical for a brand that is meant to feel confident and quietly warm.

The palette

Tints come from opacity

There are no other named colors. Borders, muted text, hover washes and focus states are one of the eight colors at a lower opacity, written as tokens so they stay consistent.

--tint-ink-4   …  --tint-ink-64      /* borders, washes, muted text */
--tint-paper-8 …  --tint-paper-80     /* the same, on dark bands        */
--tint-coral-12, --tint-coral-24
--tint-aqua-16,  --tint-aqua-32

Contrast

Body text is Ink. Text on a coral fill is white (--text-on-coral); white on coral is 2.9:1, so keep coral-fill labels at 14px medium or larger and never use them for body-length copy. Coral text on Paper fails at 2.49:1, which is why coral is a fill and a display color only. Paper on Astronaut or Reef is AAA.

Do

Use a white label on a coral button, at 14px medium or larger.

Don’t

Set a coral paragraph, or small coral text, on Paper.

Do

Make a hover state with an Ink wash at 4 to 8% opacity.

Don’t

Invent a new named color for a one-off state.

Status without red and green

The palette has no error or warning colors, by design. Validation and status states pair Ink text with an icon, so meaning never depends on color alone.

Dark mode

Light is primary and stable. Dark mode exists as tokens under [data-theme="dark"] (Ink background, Paper text, paper-opacity tints) but is still being refined, so build light first.
